In quantitative neurophysics and peripheral bioelectronic modeling, signal propagation and ion channel gating throughout the extensive somatic nervous system are governed by electrostatic potential fields, spatial charge vectors, and dipole-dipole interactions [Gabriel, 1996; Sartori & Lloyd, 2014]. 1\. Spatial Field Lengths and Dipole Vectors of Neuronal Membrane Boundaries
----------------------------------------------------------------------------

A molecule's permanent dipole moment functions as a spatial vector pointing from the center of positive charge to the center of negative charge. This spatial separation defines the electrostatic field length ($r$):

$$\mu = \sum_{i} q_i \cdot \mathbf{r}_i$$

Where $q_i$ represents the partial atomic charge of each constituent atom, and $\mathbf{r}_i$ is its position vector.

-   Phospholipid Head Group Vectors: The structural architecture of the neuronal plasma membrane is dominated by phosphatidylcholine and phosphatidylethanolamine groups. The polar zwitterionic head groups generate intense, permanent localized dipoles perpendicular to the membrane plane. This spatial configuration creates a sharp dielectric boundary layer that interfaces directly with external ionic currents.
-   Induced Nodal Tensors: Along myelinated peripheral nerves, localized gaps known as the Nodes of Ranvier lack insulating lipid sheets. These uninsulated regions display a concentrated distribution of voltage-gated sodium ($\text{Na}^+$) and potassium ($\text{K}^+$) channels. When an action potential arrives, it triggers intense, localized electrostatic fields that induce rapid changes in the polarizability tensor of surrounding water molecules and mobile trace mineral complexes, drawing key elemental co-factors directly into the channel gates [Gabriel, 1996; VirusTC, 2026].

3\. Transneuronal Cable Properties and Long-Range Electrostatic Draw
--------------------------------------------------------------------

When trace element complexes or bioactive molecules travel systemically, their delivery to peripheral nerves is directed by long-range electrostatic kinetic draw and cable properties [Venkatesh & Raghavan, 2004]. The physical interaction energy ($V$) between the circulating compound's dipole vector ($\mu_1$) and the axonal target matrix ($\mu_2$) operates inversely to the cube of the distance ($1/r^3$):

$$V = \frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0\varepsilon_r} \cdot \frac{1}{r^3} \left[ \mathbf{\mu}_1 \cdot \mathbf{\mu}_2 - 3(\mathbf{\mu}_1 \cdot \hat{\mathbf{r}})(\mathbf{\mu}_2 \cdot \hat{\mathbf{r}}) \right]$$

A. Cable Property Conduction Math
---------------------------------

Nerves function as lossy biophysical cables. The spatial spread of an electrical wave down an axon is limited by the space constant ($\lambda$), which depends on the ratio of the internal axonal conductivity ($\sigma_{\text{int}}$) to the external membrane insulation:

$$\lambda = \sqrt{\frac{r_{\text{axoon}} \cdot \sigma_{\text{int}}}{2 \cdot \sigma_{\text{mem}}}}$$

When a patient experiences systemic clinical depletion or severe neuropathic stress, the membrane conductivity ($\sigma_{\text{mem}}$) increases due to localized lipid degradation or ion leakage [VirusTC, 2026]. This change shortens the space constant ($\lambda$), causing the action potential to fade prematurely along the nerve trunk.

B. Resonant Kinetic Target Drawing
----------------------------------

The drop in the nerve's space constant alters the local relative permittivity ($\varepsilon_r = 32.5$ at 2.45 GHz). This shift modifies the regional electrostatic charge density, strengthening the localized field gradient. Incoming divalent mineral complexes or polarized plant isolates align with these altered spatial field lengths. They are drawn down this kinetic gradient, concentrating at the node of injury rather than dispersing randomly through non-target tissues.

C. Voltage Stabilization Across the Somatic Grid
------------------------------------------------

At close ranges, the spatial frequencies of the arriving complexes converge with the structural binding pockets of voltage-gated ion channels. The field forces minimize ($V \rightarrow \text{minimum}$), locking the regulatory co-factors into the channel matrix. This interaction stabilizes the baseline membrane resistance, plugs leaky ion pores, and restores normal action potential velocities across the somatic grid [VirusTC, 2026]. This physical alignment prevents random, painful firing along peripheral paths and preserves the system's structural integrity under specialized clinical frameworks [U.S. Food and Drug Administration, 2023; VirusTC, 2026].
